## Who this is for

Trackly is built for software engineering teams at Series A-C SaaS companies, typically 20 to 200 people. If you are searching for project management software for an architecture, construction, or civil engineering firm, this is not that. Trackly does not do blueprints, site scheduling, or field reporting. It does sprint planning, issue tracking, async collaboration, and OKR tracking for people who write and ship code.

If you are an engineering manager, head of operations, or CTO trying to keep a growing engineering org aligned without adding process overhead, keep reading.

## What engineering teams actually need from project management software

Most project management tools were built for general work coordination first and adapted for engineering later. That shows up as friction: sprint boards that do not map to how engineers actually plan cycles, OKRs tracked in a separate tool from the work that ladders up to them, and integrations that connect but do not sync cleanly with Jira, GitHub, or GitLab.

Trackly is built in the other direction. Here is what that covers.

### Sprint planning
Plan sprints against real team capacity, not a spreadsheet estimate. Carry over unfinished work automatically, and see burndown by team or by individual without exporting to another tool.

### Issue tracking
Track bugs and feature work from report to resolution, with statuses that match how engineering teams actually triage: not just "to do, doing, done," but severity, ownership, and linked pull requests.

### Async team collaboration
For distributed and hybrid engineering teams, decisions and status updates live in the task, not in a Slack thread that gets lost. Async standups and written updates replace meetings without losing visibility.

### Trackly vs. Linear
Linear is fast and its interface is clean, and it is genuinely good at issue and cycle management. What it does not do is connect that work to broader engineering operations: OKRs, cross-functional roadmaps, or team-level productivity reporting live outside Linear. Trackly keeps issue tracking as fast, and adds the OKR and reporting layer Linear does not have.

### Trackly vs. Asana
Asana is built to span an entire company, engineering included. That breadth means engineering-specific workflows, like PR-linked issues or capacity-aware sprint planning, are adapted rather than native. Trackly is built for engineering teams first, so those workflows do not require workarounds.

### Trackly vs. ClickUp, monday.com, and Notion
All three are general-purpose and highly customizable, which means engineering teams can configure something close to what they need, but have to build it themselves. Trackly ships with sprint planning, issue tracking, and OKR tracking already configured for how engineering teams work, out of the box.
