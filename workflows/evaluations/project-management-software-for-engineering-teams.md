# Content Evaluation: "project management software for engineering teams"

**Draft:** [workflows/drafts/project-management-software-for-engineering-teams.md](../drafts/project-management-software-for-engineering-teams.md)
**Status: NOT READY TO PUBLISH** — blocked on unverified claims, not on structure or tone.

## Overall assessment
Structure, intent match, and differentiation strategy from the brief are all executed correctly. The page cannot go live as-is because the Proof and Built-by-teams-like-yours sections are placeholders, and several other claims are unverified. Publishing with invented statistics or customer names would be a factual-accuracy and trust failure, not a style issue — fix those before anything else.

## Search intent match
Strong. The intent-qualifier section directly addresses the AEC-vs-software split identified in the SERP analysis, and the comparison table/FAQ serve both the vendor-page searcher and the listicle-style comparison searcher in one asset, as the brief intended.

## Content depth and structure
Meets the brief's outline: hero, qualifier, five topic-cluster sections, comparison module, proof, customer proof, integrations, FAQ, CTA. OKR tracking gets its own subsection as planned, closing the gap no competitor addresses. No structural changes needed once the sourcing issues below are resolved.

## Differentiation vs. competitors
Strong on strategy: names Linear, Asana, ClickUp, monday.com, and Notion directly, which the brief flagged as safe since none defend this query today. The Linear and Asana comparison paragraphs make specific, non-generic claims about what each tool does and does not do, rather than vague "we're better" statements.

## Fact-check results

**Cannot verify, must fix before publish:**
1. **Proof section** — currently a placeholder with no metrics. Publishing without real, sourced numbers here would be the single biggest trust risk on the page. Needs actual product or customer data before this section can be written.
2. **Built by teams like yours** — placeholder, no real customer names. Do not fill with invented company names or fabricated quotes. Needs real customer sign-off.
3. **OKR tracking feature claims** (hero-adjacent section and FAQ answer) — states OKRs "connect directly to the sprints and issues that move them." This is asserted as fact but flagged in the draft as unverified. Confirm against actual product behavior before publishing; if the feature doesn't work exactly this way, the claim needs to change.
4. **Engineering team productivity metrics claim** — "see cycle time, throughput, and sprint completion rate by team" is stated as a native capability. Confirm these are actually surfaced natively, not aspirational.
5. **Integrations list and count** — draft states Jira, GitHub, GitLab, Slack. Confirm this is the complete and current list before publishing; do not add a specific integration count (e.g., matching Asana's "260+") unless it is Trackly's real, current number.

**Competitor claims (strictest standard, per evaluator rules):**
- Linear: "fast and its interface is clean... does not do... OKRs, cross-functional roadmaps, or team-level productivity reporting" — consistent with what the competitor-analyst brief found in third-party listicles (GoodDay, TheDigitalPM). Acceptable, sourced indirectly via the SERP research already done.
- Asana: "breadth means engineering-specific workflows... are adapted rather than native" — this is an inference from Asana's own page structure (general feature set applied to an engineering vertical page), not a directly sourced quote. Reasonable characterization but soften slightly, e.g. "engineering teams often need to adapt these workflows," rather than stating it as settled fact about every Asana customer.
- ClickUp/monday.com/Notion: characterized generically as "general-purpose" and requiring self-configuration. This is a fair, low-risk characterization consistent with public positioning of all three tools; no specific claim needs a citation.

## Tone consistency
Matches [tone-of-voice.md](../../tone-of-voice.md) well: short sentences, claims are structured to lead with the point, no em dashes present, none of the banned words (seamless, robust, game-changer, synergy, leverage-as-verb, powerful, innovative) appear anywhere in the draft. No changes needed here.

One soft spot: the "What engineering teams actually need" intro paragraph is the closest thing to generic scene-setting in the draft ("Most project management tools were built for general work coordination first..."). It's short and earns its place, but keep an eye on this pattern in future sections, it's the one place that reads slightly closer to "average of the internet" than the rest of the page.

## CTR assessment (title/meta)
Title and meta both lead with "software engineering teams," correctly filtering AEC search intent as the brief recommended. Meta description names three competitors (Linear, Asana, ClickUp) directly, which the brief identified as unclaimed space since no competitor names competitors in their own title tags. This is a differentiated, intent-aligned title/meta pair. No changes recommended.
